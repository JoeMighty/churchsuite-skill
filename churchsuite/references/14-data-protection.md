# Data protection and GDPR

ChurchSuite Ltd is a data processor/service provider. The church or charity is the data controller. Always suggest consulting the organisation's data protection officer or the ICO before changing how personal data is processed. This file is practical guidance, not legal advice.

## Where ChurchSuite helps

- **Data Protection statement (privacy notice)** upload in Administrator, Profile. Required for the "Forget me" function to appear. A data protection contact is set in Profile, Contacts and receives notifications.
- **Data protection checkbox** on all forms collecting personal data, with customisable Help text.
- **Consent**: My Consent requests, Ongoing consent article (474), changes logged and an Ongoing Consent key date recorded. Pending status holds people awaiting consent.
- **Privacy settings**: members control visibility of their details to other members. Parent/carer controls for children.
- **Right to be forgotten**: "Forget me" or Delete account in My ChurchSuite (Privacy page), also reachable from unsubscribe and My Consent forms. Nothing is auto deleted. A notification goes to the data protection contact, a Right to be Forgotten key date and change logs are added. The contact then follows the church's process, bearing in mind other lawful bases (legitimate interest, safeguarding, financial records, electoral roll). For US accounts the Forget me link is hidden.
- **Delete vs archive**: Archive keeps the record inactive and unassigns tags. Delete permanently removes data and blocks My ChurchSuite access (articles 49, 462).
- **Retention** (article 155): daily automated backups kept 30 days, deleted data log, email errors purged after 6 months, login logs after 3 years, visitor children after the retention setting.
- **Security**: MFA, module passwords, per module permissions, User Groups, time limited Support Team access, encryption at rest in UK data centres.
- **API**: enabling API access notifies the data protection contact. API v2 can read personal data. Treat client secrets as passwords. Disable API access for unused users.

## Good practice questions (from the official Getting Started)

- Do people know what personal data you hold and why? Lawful basis and privacy notice up to date?
- Does only appropriate staff have access? Are permissions right?
- Is the data retention policy current, and have unneeded records been purged?
- Is training and confidentiality refreshed?
- Are default privacy and communication options appropriate?
- Do data capture processes (visitor cards, web forms) show privacy notices?
- Does the safeguarding policy reflect how children's and vulnerable adults' data is handled?
